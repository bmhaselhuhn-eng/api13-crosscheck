The rule

SAME_BAND_RULE version 1.3-draft, sections 2 to 7.

2. The question being answered

Are these two crops renderings of one photographic exposure of one physical band?

The question is not whether they look alike, and not whether they look suspicious. Two lanes loaded with the same lysate and imaged in the same exposure are two bands, so they are different. One band photographed once and printed twice is the same, however much the two printings differ.

The question is about the image, not about misconduct. A pair can be the same image for entirely innocent reasons, and judging that is not this rule's job.

3. Positive evidence, in order of weight

A shared accident is the only strong evidence. Accidents are random, so two images are unlikely to share one unless they share an origin.

  • A dust speck, scratch, bubble or hair in the same position relative to the band. This decides the pair on its own when the mark is off the band, in the background, where nothing about the biology would put it in both images.
  • The same irregularity in the band outline. A nick, a notch or a smear tail that matches in shape and in position, not just in kind.
  • Matching background grain or compression blocks. Two renderings of one file carry the same noise. Two exposures do not.
  • A difference panel that is near-white everywhere, background included.

The gap between overall similarity and fine-detail similarity does not decide a pair. This was tried, and it is recorded so it is not tried again. The conformance checker briefly flagged pairs whose whole-image similarity was more than 0.12 above their fine-detail similarity, on the reasoning that shape agreeing while grain disagrees is exactly the case that is not enough. Two pairs have since been adjudicated, and they fall in the wrong order: kp002, with a gap of 0.145, is not a duplicate, and kp048, with a gap of 0.164, is. The cutoff was withdrawn. The checker still sorts pairs by the gap, because deciding what to look at first costs nothing, and it decides nothing with it.

Evidence that is not enough on its own, starting with what misleads most often:

  • Similar overall shape, width or intensity. Bands from one gel look alike because they come from one gel.
  • A high correlation score (NCC). That score is what put the pair in front of you, so it cannot also be the reason for the verdict.
  • A difference panel that is near-white inside the band only, with red around the outline. That is the saturation trap in §5, and it is the most convincing wrong answer there is.

4. Negative evidence

  • Red, band-shaped structure running through the difference panel.
  • Different lane spacing, band count, or position relative to neighboring bands.
  • Backgrounds that differ in texture where the bands agree.
  • Shapes that agree with no shared accident anywhere, at any zoom.

A pair with no shared accident is different by default. Missing positive evidence is not a reason for unsure. It is the negative answer. unsure is for pairs where the evidence is there but cannot be read, not for pairs where it is absent.

5. The saturation trap

Recorded because it produced the one cross-paper candidate of 4 August 2026, and that pair was almost certainly not a duplicate.

When both bands are saturated, the inside of each band is a flat, clipped plateau that carries no information, and any two clipped plateaus match there trivially. The difference panel then shows white across the band and red around its whole outline. That looks like a strong match and means the opposite.

Rule: when the difference panel is white inside the band and red around the outline, the inside of the band is not evidence. Judge the pair on the background and the outline only. If the shared accident is not in the background, the answer is different.

5a. How often this can arise, measured 9 August 2026

The rule above was written from one adjudicated pair. To see how often its condition arises, the 7,513 cross-paper candidate pairs with a correlation (NCC) of 0.995 or more were matched to the saturation classifier's own verdicts.

Within a retrieved pair, saturation goes together strongly: association odds ratio 10.62. Both crops are flagged as saturated in 0.1702 of the 1,733 pairs where both have a verdict, against 0.0732 expected if the two crops were independent at the pooled rate of 0.2706. That is 2.32 times independence, with a 95% CI on the both-flagged share of [0.1523, 0.1881]. Pairs with neither crop flagged are 1.18 times as common as independence predicts, and pairs with one crop flagged fall to 0.51 times, so the association runs both ways rather than pulling toward saturation.

The matcher does not pick out saturated bands more than others. Retrieved crops are flagged at 0.2635, against 0.3338 for the corpus, a rate ratio of 0.789 with a 95% CI clustered by paper of [0.730, 0.851], over 2,216 and 35,703 papers. The ratio stays below 1 at every specificity from 0.80 to 1.00. An earlier suspicion that the 0.995 operating point partly acted as a saturation detector is withdrawn.

The two results do not conflict. The threshold does not select saturated material, and once a pair is in front of you, the odds that both band interiors are clipped are roughly ten times what independence would give. So §5 is a rule that applies often, not a corner case.

What this does not show. Going together is association, not mechanism: two bands of one protein at one exposure share saturation status for ordinary reasons, and nothing here separates that from the trap. It also rests on 1,733 of the 7,513 pairs. Of the rest, 3,234 dropped out because one crop has no saturation verdict at all, which a coverage check traced to a gap in the order the data were built, not to anything about the bands. The remaining pairs dropped out because the classifier deferred. Retrieved crops are deferred at 42.2%, against 34.6% for the corpus, a 1.22-fold excess that is the one route by which this measurement could still be biased.

§5a is not cited until that route is measured. The design was registered here before any scoring, so it could not be chosen after seeing the answer. Round deferral01, draw seed 20260809: 180 retrieved crops, 60 each from deferred, called clean and called flagged, at most one crop per paper, with the stratum kept in the key and left off the sheet. Each rater sees the crops in an independent random order, set from the seed and the round name together, so two raters given the same 180 crops meet them in different orders and share no blind ids. Fatigue depends on position, and raters who meet the same hard band at the same position agree there partly for reasons that are not the rubric. The sheet is built on the lab's saturation-scoring instrument at rubric v1.8-final, so these labels sit on the same scale as every other round. The blinding check found nothing.

The sheet builder for this round was checked against a graded version, not assumed to match it. The builder in use differs from the version the test harness graded by exactly one code token out of 3,500: one word in one question, changed on 9 August from "indigo" to "dashed" after a change to the overlay display left the question naming a color the display no longer shows. The graded version scores 1.000, and passes on every run, on all 27 test cases. So the layout producing these labels is the layout the harness graded, and the one difference is text a scorer reads, not a quantity anything computes.

The two called strata are the calibration: they show whether the scorer and the classifier read saturation the same way. Only if they do does the deferred rate mean anything, and only then is the pooled rate of 0.2706 revised. If the deferred rate comes out near the called rate, the odds ratio above stands as written. If it does not, §5a is rewritten before it is cited.

6. Categories that are not reuse

These are removed before labeling and should never reach a sheet. If one does, mark it notband or different and say so.

CategoryHow to recognize it
Detection seamSame paper, same figure, two crops covering the same spot: one physical band found by both detection passes. The tools flag it.
Adjacent lanesSame figure, band numbers within 3 of each other. Lanes of one blot.
Duplicate figure fileSame paper, figure names that differ only by language, such as "Figure1" and "Figura1", or "gf01" and "gf01en". Bilingual journals publish the figure twice. 14 groups across 7 papers as of 4 August 2026.
FurnitureArrows, labels, scale bars, panel letters. Standard symbols really are identical across papers, because they are the same symbol. Crops with a high band number within a figure are more often these.

7. Confidence, and the agreement target

Every label carries one of these:

  • confirmed: either one decisive indicator, or two independent weaker indicators, each identified and pointed to. Decisive: a §3.1 accident off the band (dust, scratch, bubble, hair), or a §3.4 difference panel near-white everywhere, background included. Weaker: a §3.2 outline irregularity that matches in shape and position, and §3.3 matching grain or compression blocks. §3.3 and §3.4 are one signal (a panel blank everywhere is what matching grain produces), so they never count as two. A §3.2 nick plus §3.3 grain counts as two.
  • probable: same, but with only one weaker indicator (a nick alone, or grain or compression blocks alone), or the difference panel and background agree with no single accident that can be named, and nothing contradicts. Kept and reported, never used in a published number.
  • unsure: evidence is present but cannot be read. This is a real answer, and it is recorded as one.
  • refuted: labeled a duplicate, then adjudicated not to be one. The row stays in the file and is left out of every positive set. Deleting it would destroy the only record of the disagreement, and adjudicated negatives are the most useful rows there are for calibrating anything: they are the cases where the answer is known and the first reading was wrong. kp002 is the first.

Disagreement between two readings is not the same as error in either. Of the two pairs that changed on the 4 August blinded re-read, kp002 was an error in the original reading and kp048 was an error in the re-read. A 10% disagreement rate over twenty pairs therefore points to about 5% error in the set, not 10%. Report both, and do not quote the disagreement rate as if it were the error rate.

Only confirmed rows enter a set that carries a published number. probable rows are kept and reported separately, because a set that quietly mixes the two cannot be audited.

Target: 95% of calls reproduced on a blinded re-read, against the 90% measured with no rule. Below 90%, the rule has not helped and needs revising rather than more labeling. Measure it with the re-read agreement check on every round, not once. Under 1.3, it is measured again before any pair is adjudicated under 1.3.
